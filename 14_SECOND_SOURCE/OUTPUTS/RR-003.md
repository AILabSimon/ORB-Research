## K1 — THE QUALIFYING RETURN

### The short answer
**Do not tighten the return threshold. Split the population.**

V2 currently has one arm where the sources have **two different entry models**, and the
one-bar-touch trades are not a defect in the retest model — they are mostly the *other*
model, mislabelled. Max teaches both, explicitly, in the same video.

### The evidence

**(a) Max teaches a retest entry, and every description of it is multi-candle.**
| Quote | Video |
|---|---|
| "close outside of orb, **retest, retest**, breakout, the higher high … this is your entry" | V8 14:52 |
| "the **second retest candle** that failed and closed outside of orb, the second this candle made a newer low, that's the retest confirmation and this was your entry" | V8 11:50 |
| "the retest is always the best way to go … **retest, doji retest**, usually could pump to a newer high" | V12 27:23 |
| "breakout, **test**, secondary entry … this is where you would double dip" | V12 06:46 |
| CeeWilli: "Price breaks above the orb, **pulls back** and then gives us our rejection" | C2 03:34 |
| VRC-01: an **8-bar** pullback with six closes back inside | C1, ES1! 28 May 2026 |

**A one-bar wick to the boundary is never called a retest anywhere in either corpus.**
The word is doubled — "retest, retest", "retest, doji retest" — and ordinal — "the
*second* retest candle". That is three independent explicit statements from two videos.

**(b) Max ALSO teaches a no-retest entry, and says so when asked directly.**
Audience question, verbatim in the transcript: *what do you do if it breaks out and
doesn't retest?*
> V12 36:39 — "if you're waiting for a **two candle close**, or you waiting for
> **retest**, or are you waiting for **market structure** — there's a couple different
> ways to do it … and they all have their own risk reward."
> V12 37:09 — "**you can enter right here on this close. You don't have to wait for
> this.** … **You can enter on any close outside of orb** … you can enter here, here,
> here, here … **There's no correct answer. Just make sure that you have your risk
> reward.**"
> V12 05:44 — two ways to long: "one, a **retracement back down to orb and then a
> bounce up**; or two, with this candle not really having any selling pressure, a
> **continuation right to the upside**."
(This is R-037, already on the record: *there is no single correct entry — stated by
the source*.)

### The ruling
Your 58% "return on the very next bar" population is not the retest model failing a
threshold. It is the **continuation model**, which is separately and explicitly taught,
being scored as if it were the retest model. Testing them as one arm guarantees that
neither is measured.

**RULE R1 — qualifying retest (retest arm only).**
The boundary must be interacted with on **at least two separate 1-minute candles**
after the break candle and before the qualifying close.
*Interaction* at bar *i*, long case: `low_i <= ORH <= high_i` — the bar's range contains
the boundary. (Short: `low_i <= ORL <= high_i`.) The two interacting bars need not be
consecutive. The qualifying close itself is not one of them.

This is **ordinal, not numeric**. There is no threshold to search: the count comes from
"retest, retest" and "the second retest candle". The mechanical minimum becomes 4 bars
(break, interact, interact, qualifying close) instead of 3, and the one-bar-wick
population is excluded **by construction**, not by tuning.

**RULE R2 — the continuation arm is a separate, legitimate model.**
No return required. Entry on a close beyond the boundary after the break
(V12 37:09, V12 05:44 branch two). Run it as its own arm with its own economics.
**We have never tested this model as such**, and on your numbers it is the majority of
what V2 has been measuring.

**Report R1 and R2 separately, end to end.** If they are pooled, the pooled number
describes neither.

### What I am NOT ruling, and why
I am not ruling that R1 is "Max's rule". He says outright there is no single correct
entry (V12 37:09) and that the alternatives "all have their own risk reward". R1 is the
minimum structure the *retest* branch is always described with. Anything beyond that —
a minimum bar count above two, a depth of pullback, a time window — has **no source
support** and would be parameter mining. Do not add one.

## K2 — FILL vs INVALIDATION COLLISION

**Ruling: not a collision. It is faithful as implemented. Report it; do not patch it.**

> V12 05:44 — "**Anytime we see a close back inside of orb anywhere**, that's an
> invalidation and you can simply exit the trade."
> V8 14:52 — "close outside of orb, retest, retest, breakout, the higher high … this is
> your entry. **The break back into orb is where you close your trade.**"

"Anywhere" is unqualified and there is no grace period anywhere in the corpus. A bar
that fills you at its open and closes back inside **is** an invalidation by the stated
rule. Your 49.4%-within-one-bar figure is therefore a **finding about the setup** — the
qualifying close is frequently the last bar of the move — not an implementation error.
It is also exactly what R1 predicts you would see when the "retest" was a one-bar wick.
**Re-measure it under R1 before concluding anything from it.**

One defensible alternative exists and should be a **secondary arm only**: treat the
fill bar as part of the entry event and start invalidation checks at the following bar
(R-049, "you wait for things to play out"). Do not make it the base case — the source
wording is "anywhere", and softening it to improve a number is the failure mode this
programme exists to avoid.

## K3 — TARGET WHEN NO PRIOR-SESSION EXTREME LIES BEYOND ENTRY

**Ruling: there is no target. Hold to invalidation. This is Max's continuation rule,
not a fallback we are inventing.**

> V12 29:25 — "Here's your trim, here's your exit. **You ride it until the
> invalidation.**"
> V12 29:57 — "go long until it's invalidated … once you get the invalidations then you
> have your confirmation to get out." (R-045, EXPLICIT and REPEATED.)

Max attaches **no price target at all** to the continuation trade. CeeWilli's target
menu is also wider than prior-session extremes: "Asian high or … the 15-minute
liquidity or all the way to the previous day high" (C1 05:07) and an unfilled new-week
opening gap (C1 16:18). SPEC-12 implemented only the narrowest member of that menu.

**⚠ Flag this one and treat it with suspicion.** You report that the no-target subset
carries all the apparent gross (+0.2016R vs −0.0166R). The faithful ruling and the
profitable subset therefore coincide, which is precisely the shape a motivated
reading takes. I am ruling on the source wording alone — R-045 is explicit and
repeated and predates any of these numbers — but the coincidence should make both of
us sceptical. Report the no-target arm with its own confidence interval and do not let
it carry a headline.

## E1 — STOP VARIANT A IS DEGENERATE. THE SOURCE SUPPLIES THE FIX.

You found R < 0.0002 × price on 20.5%/26.2% of trades, max 699R. **Max identifies this
exact failure and prescribes a floor for it** — R-050, which V2 did not implement:

> V13 05:57 — "**The previous 20 candles had 15 to 20 point wicks, but you got a
> five-point stop-loss and you don't think this is going to wick.** You need to do
> either two things here. One, you need to check your size and go down maybe to three
> or four micros instead of a mini and **allow that 25 point stop loss**. Or two, you
> need to just let the trade breathe."

**RULE R3 — MAX-S1 is floored, not raw.**
`stop_distance = max( |entry − reclaim-candle extreme| , wick_floor )`
where `wick_floor` = the upper bound of the wick length distribution over the previous
**20** 1-minute candles (his number, not ours — use the max, or state which statistic
you use and why).

Variant A as tested was **not** Max's stop; it was half of it. "Not mechanisable" should
be withdrawn as a conclusion about the source and re-tested with the floor in place.
If it is still degenerate with the floor, *that* is a finding about Max.

## ONE THING V2 DID NOT IMPLEMENT

Your §B describes all four arms as: *ENTRY SIGNAL = first later 1m close beyond the
boundary → FILL at the next bar's open.* That is **CeeWilli's** trigger. **Max's
trigger was not built.** Max enters on a **stop order at the reclaim candle's extreme**,
filled only if the next bar trades through it:
> V10 24:18 — "as soon as I see a candle flip up or **print through the previous
> candle**, I'm going long."
> V11 08:34 — "I want to see the print through — **the next candle's wick hit 308 and
> then go**."

Consequences: it is an extra filter (the next bar must make a new extreme), it produces
**non-fills** (R-039 — acceptance test H10, which cannot have passed), and it changes
the fill price from the next bar's open to a level inside that bar. Arms A and B
currently differ only in the stop, so the Max **entry** model is still untested.
