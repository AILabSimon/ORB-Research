## 3. The one thing worth taking

### CW-01 — liquidity context as a prospective break-vs-fake-out discriminator

> C3 01:31–02:02 — "Why do these fakeouts happen? Usually … because we're trading into some sort of
> liquidity. And when I say liquidity, I mean stuff like swing highs and swing lows … it's just where
> people have their stop orders. **So if a clean, obvious liquidity [is] sitting right above the orb,
> it's most likely going to be a fake out. And it's easily avoidable.**"

Formalised by him at C3 17:18:
- **Expect a fake-out** when an unswept swing high/low sits just beyond the ORB edge.
- **Expect a real breakout** when "liquidity was already swept **before the orb formed**", and the
  break runs with the HTF trend.

**Why this is worth one run.** It is the only prospective, pre-entry, objectively codeable
discriminator in the source. It is evaluable **at 09:45, before any entry**, from data entirely in
the past: the position of prior swing extremes relative to the ORB boundary, and whether they were
taken out before 09:45. There is **no look-ahead** — unlike the TSUGI-03 edge-interaction variable,
which needed a candle's close and cost −0.155R to extract. **Extraction cost here is zero.**

**Why it will probably still fail.** It is a filter on the existing entry geometry, which the 17 Sep
position explicitly says is not what the family needs, and **D-031 — every pattern tested on gross
collapses to zero** — is the broad closure that most likely swallows it.

**But the narrow closure does not cover it, and this was checked rather than assumed.** D-038's ten
pre-entry features were: *reached the edge · direction · W_rel · imp_R · cost/R · latency · W ·
day-of-week · straddle · outside-fraction*. Every one is a property of the range itself, of the
break's own path, or of the calendar. **None is prior-session structural context** — the position of
unswept swing extremes relative to the ORB boundary. `straddle` and `outside-fraction` describe how
price behaves around the edge once the range exists; that is a different variable.

Two further reasons D-038 does not settle it:
- It screened against **full-stop-versus-not**, a *loss* predictor. CW-01 predicts **which side
  resolves** — a different target.
- CW-01 is knowable at 09:45 from prior-session data alone, so it sits in a different information
  class from the post-entry path features D-038 concluded are "not knowable at entry".

**Recommended form if run:** classify each event at 09:45 by whether an unswept swing extreme from
the prior session sits within some band beyond the ORB edge. Test on **gross** first. If gross is
zero, stop — no cost model needed, and it joins D-031.

## 4. A direct conflict worth stating

The programme's hardest finding is that **the entry price must be the ORB edge**, because the
weakest possible confirmation — one minute — already costs more than the edge is worth.

This source says the opposite, and is specific about it:

> C2 13:20 — of entering the break without waiting: **"Do not recommend doing that on futures though
> because you will get [screwed] most likely."**

He reserves the un-confirmed entry for options, where the payoff is premium percentage rather than an
R multiple against a price stop — a context in which the programme's cost arithmetic does not apply.

Both positions cannot be right for futures. The programme's is measured on 9,360 trades with
reconstructed spreads; his is asserted over screenshots. That asymmetry is decisive on current
evidence, but the conflict is recorded rather than dissolved, because it is the same disagreement
that separates what Max *teaches* from what Max *does*, and it now has a third independent voice on
the confirmation side.

## 6. Position

- **No change to CM-C1 or to the 17 Sep position.** Nothing here reopens the retest family.
- **The request that prompted this review — a materially different entry geometry — is not met.**

### Measured against the programme's own reopening criterion

The DECISION_LOG states the bar explicitly: the family reopens only on *"a materially larger raw
edge from a different entry geometry (not a filter on this one), or a cost structure below ~0.046R
per trade."* Everything this source offers is either the same geometry or a **filter on it**.
By that criterion, **nothing here qualifies to reopen the family** — CW-01 included.

That is the honest headline and it should not be softened. A 0.046R signal cannot pay a 0.071R toll,
and a filter changes which trades are taken, not the toll.

### What CW-01 is actually for

Not a candidate. A **gap-closer in the discriminant search**, which was declared closed on ten
pre-entry features. CW-01 is a *structural context* feature — the position of unswept prior swing
extremes relative to the ORB boundary at 09:45 — and if the ten features were candle-shape and
path-based, this class was not among them. It costs nothing to extract (no bar close, no look-ahead)
and can be screened on **gross only**:

- If gross is ~zero, it joins D-031 and the discriminant search is closed on a broader basis than
  before, which is a cleaner statement than the current one.
- If gross is materially non-zero, that is genuinely new information and the Analyst decides what it
  means. It would still have to clear the 0.071R toll to matter economically.

**Run it only if the Analyst judges the marginal certainty worth the run.** Under mandate section 25
(do not raise a request whose answer cannot change a decision) the case is borderline, and the
Research Agent's own prior is that it returns zero. It is recorded, not pressed.

- **Do not re-test anything in the confirmation family** on the strength of this source. It is the
  most confirmation-heavy of the three authors reviewed and sits on the wrong side of a finding that
  has now been measured four independent ways.
- **The options half of this source is untestable here** and should not be carried as an open item.
