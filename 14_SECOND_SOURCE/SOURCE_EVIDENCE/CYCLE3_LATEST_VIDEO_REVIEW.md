### On the MVG — a technical correction worth recording
V14 13:50 gives, for the first time, a *mechanism*:
> *"It's institutional data lagging from the data provider. The CME sends the information out to
> TradingView, Tradeify, WealthCharts … and sometimes that data lags because of a massive order block
> during the time stamp … that lag is what I call an MVG. **I'm pretty sure I invented this.**"*

**This explanation is wrong.** A candle with no wick on one side is an ordinary price gap/imbalance
between consecutive bars — the standard fair-value-gap construct, which long predates him. There is no
data-feed lag mechanism; CME data is timestamped at the exchange and a "lag" would not manifest as a
missing wick. The *marker* may still have whatever value it has as a price feature; the *story* about
why it exists is not correct, and the originality claim is not supportable. Recorded so that the
marker is not adopted on the strength of a false mechanism.

# 6. THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS FOR THE ANALYST

**N-2 looks like it fixes the cost arithmetic. It does not, and the programme has already measured why.**

The closing arithmetic was: median R **9.3 points**, S2 realistic round trip **1.335 points**,
breakeven mean cost/R **+0.2806R** against raw gross **+0.1100R** — a 2.55× shortfall.

N-2 says the taught stop should be **25 points**, not 9. That changes cost/R to roughly
1.335 / 25 ≈ **0.053R**, which sits right at the *"cost structure below ~0.046R per trade"* threshold
the DECISION_LOG names as one of only two ways to reopen the family. On its face this is the single
most promising thing in six weeks of source research.

**It fails on a result the programme already holds.** RESEARCH_STATE finding 3: *the edge is inversely
related to tradability* — raw gross **+0.110R** (all) → **+0.063R** (R≥5) → **+0.031R** (R≥10) →
**−0.034R (R≥20)**. At the R the wick-sized stop implies, **the gross edge is already negative**.
Widening the stop buys down cost/R more slowly than it destroys the signal.

So N-2 is not a rescue. It is a **confirmation that the closure is robust against the most obvious
remaining objection** — "you were using too tight a stop". The source's own preferred stop is wider
than the one tested, and the programme has already measured that wider band and found it worse. That
is worth recording explicitly, because it is exactly the challenge a reviewer would raise.

**N-4 is cheap and objective and is the only new filter worth a run** — ORB break *and* previous-day
extreme broken in the same event, with a claimed 85–87% hit rate. It is a filter, so by the stated
criterion it cannot reopen the family; but it is a specific number that can be falsified in one pass,
and a claimed 85–87% against a measured ~51% base rate is a large enough gap to be worth closing on
the record. Test on **gross** first.

**N-10 is the only item that points outside the exhausted family.** V14 17:42 — *"you have a … better
chance of success if you **open a trade at 3:59 p.m. and close it at 9:31** than holding a trade from
9:31 to 3:59. **The market moves significantly better overnight when it's closed** than it does during
the actual market session."* The "thousands of percent" framing is nonsense, but the underlying
overnight-versus-intraday return asymmetry is a real and well-documented equity-index phenomenon. It is
**not** an ORB strategy and it is **out of scope** for this project. It is flagged for one reason: the
programme's binding constraint is cost/R, and an overnight hold has one round trip against a much
larger move — a fundamentally different cost geometry from the one that killed every candidate here.
Recorded as an observation, not a recommendation, and not a request.
