Read this first — three things you may be expecting that are not here

BOS and CHOCH have not been researched or tested in this programme. There is no six-step trading model in this work. If a six-step methodology exists in another programme, it is not this one. What I can show you is the six-stage mechanism we actually built and measured — it is set out in section 02 and clearly labelled as ours.

No live or forward-tested trades exist. Every number in this brief is a historical simulation on 1-minute data. Nothing has been traded.

The one finding that changed everything

The first investigation failed because its stop was sized off the entry candle — about 9 index points — so a realistic round-trip cost consumed 28% of every unit of risk. Re-sizing the stop off the opening range itself raised risk per trade to 10–70 points and dropped cost to 4–18% of risk. The cost problem was a property of that one stop construction, not of opening-range trading. That insight is reusable whatever happens next.

02

The mechanism we actually test

This is our own construction, built from measured behaviour. It is not BOS/CHOCH and it is not anyone's published six-step model. Every stage is objective and codeable — no discretion, no indicators.

01
Build the range
First 30 minutes of the session, wick to wick. Fixed clock, no judgement.
02
Wait for a break
Price trades beyond the range edge. This alone is not a signal.
03
Require a completed close
A full 30-minute bar must close outside. Confirms the break was real.
04
Enter on the retest
First return to within 0.10×W of the broken edge. This is the edge.
05
Stop and trail
Stop at 0.5×W. Once +1R, trail 1R behind the high.
06
Hard time exit
Flat by 180 minutes regardless. No overnight risk.

04

Errors found, and what we corrected

Four were mine. One was raised by the research side and I accepted it. All are corrected in the record rather than explained around.

1 · Median reported as a breakeven requirement

I quoted cost as "18.8% of risk" and breakeven as "+0.290R" as though they were the same quantity. They were the median and the mean of the same ratio. Expectancy is a mean, so only the mean is a breakeven. Corrected — mean is now used throughout.

2 · Entry slippage counted twice

Half a point of entry slippage was already inside the fill price, and a full round trip was charged on top. Corrected — every cost component now applied exactly once, from an explicit dollar build-up. The corrected result was less unfavourable, not more.

3 · A claim falsified against the wrong variable

I tested "works best on large-move mornings" by sorting on opening-range width. The source meant compression — the opposite end of the variable. My result was in fact consistent with the claim and I read it as contradicting it. Corrected, then the claim was tested properly and did not hold.

4 · A filter that was noise

A "risk ≥ 15 points" screen looked like it rescued the strategy. It failed three of four robustness tests and did not replicate on the corrected build. Retired explicitly so it cannot return under a different name.

5 · Overstated scope

I wrote that the method was "falsified". We tested CFD and spot proxies, not NQ, MNQ, ES, SPX or options. Corrected wording: the reconstructed families did not produce a viable costs-adjusted strategy on proxies; source-native futures and options remain untested, not disproven. This distinction is maintained everywhere in the record now.

The three adjustments that mattered

Entry
Completed close outside the range
→ Retest of the broken edge

Measured across 5 markets: a completed close destroys information. The retest is the strongest family in every market.

Stop
Entry candle — 9 points
→ 0.5 × range width — 25.7 points

Cost falls from 28% of risk to 5%. This single change is the difference between the two tables.

Exit
Hold to invalidation
→ Trail from +1R, hard 180-min exit

Median hold falls from 3 minutes to 66. Break-even exits become a real category instead of ~0.

07

What is settled, what is not, what is unknown

Settled

  • The retest is the strongest entry in every market tested. Positive in 16 of 18 cells, significant in 9, and positive in gross terms on all five markets.
  • A completed close destroys information. A touch beats a close in 13 of 18 cells. Confirmed on four markets independently.
  • Cost is a property of the stop, not of the strategy. Range-derived stops cut cost from 28% to 4–18% of risk.
  • Instrument economics decide the outcome. GBPUSD has the largest gross effect in the panel and the worst net result.
  • 180 minutes is not a constraint. It captures 76% of the day's available move; median hold is 66 minutes.

Still to clarify

  • Does the Nasdaq candidate survive on real futures? +0.105R on assumed futures costs, −0.001R on measured CFD costs. One dataset settles it.
  • Why is holdout six times stronger than development? Unexplained. Could be regime, could be something we have not isolated.
  • Is gold real? Best 2R geometry in the panel and the cheapest costs, but 3.2 years and every interval includes zero.
  • Selection risk. 72 behavioural measurements and ~40 constructed variants were examined. One positive is what chance alone would produce.

Unknown — no data at all

  • BOS / CHOCH and any market-structure framework. Never researched, never tested. No opinion available.
  • Options. No contract data exists anywhere in our store. Nothing here is evidence for or against an options implementation.
  • True futures execution. NQ, MNQ, ES, MES, GC, MGC — none held. All index and gold results are proxies.
  • Live behaviour. Zero forward or live trades. Slippage on a real breakout fill is modelled, not observed.
  • Gold spread. No ask-side series, so gold's spread is assumed, never measured.
08

Direction from here

The recommendation is to spend money on data, not on more analysis

The only live candidate sits between +0.105R and −0.001R depending entirely on which instrument's costs apply. That gap is a missing dataset, not a modelling question. No further behavioural work, no additional market and no extra filter can close it — and I have recommended against all three.

Priority 1
NQ / MNQ 1-min history
Decides the only live candidate. Re-run the existing tests unchanged on native data; if it holds, it advances to full validation.
Priority 2
GC / MGC 1-min history
Decides gold, which has the best 2R geometry and cheapest costs in the panel and is blocked only by a short proxy sample.
Explicitly not recommended
More markets or filters
Five markets already answer the behavioural question consistently. Adding more raises selection risk without adding information.

Standing position on the existing FX control

The live FX ORB control (00:00–08:00 range, ATR buffer, opposite-side stop, 16:00 exit, roughly +0.049R) is unchanged and was never pooled with any of this work. Nothing found here justifies modifying it. The structural contrast in fact supports its design: an eight-hour range with an opposite-side stop produces a risk unit large enough to absorb FX commission — a fifteen-minute opening range does not. That is the same mechanism that explains why Nasdaq works where the S&P 500 does not.
