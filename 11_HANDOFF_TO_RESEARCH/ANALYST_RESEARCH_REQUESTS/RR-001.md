## What is needed, in priority order

### Tier 1 — unblocks the behavioural census (needed first)

1. **Range window.** Clock start time, duration, and timezone. Is it anchored to the 09:30
   America/New_York cash open, to the futures session, or to another reference? Is the range
   built from the first N minutes, or the first N bars of a stated chart timeframe?
2. **Instrument and market.** What is actually traded and shown: SPX, SPY, ES/MES, NQ/MNQ, or
   options on one of them? If options — 0DTE or dated, and is strike selection stated?
3. **Break definition.** Is the signal the first print/touch beyond the boundary, or a
   completed close beyond it? If a close — on which timeframe is that close measured
   (1m, 5m, 15m)? Is any buffer, tick offset or ATR distance stated?

### Tier 2 — shapes candidate construction (needed soon after)

4. **Confirmation.** Anything required between break and entry (volume, momentum, a retest,
   a second bar). State whether it is objective or discretionary.
5. **Strategy families.** How many structurally distinct playbooks exist in the source, and
   which is presented as primary. Flag any that are opposite in premise (continuation vs
   fade), because they must be assessed as separate candidates and never pooled.
6. **No-trade conditions.** Any stated filter, and critically whether it is determinable
   BEFORE the trade or only identifiable with hindsight.

### Tier 3 — needed before any costed or 2R claim

7. **Stop placement** as actually demonstrated, not as described in general terms.
8. **Exit / target** convention: fixed R, fixed points, opposite boundary, trailing, time-based,
   or discretionary. State if the source shows exits being managed discretionarily.
9. **Maximum trades per day** and re-entry behaviour after a first loss.
